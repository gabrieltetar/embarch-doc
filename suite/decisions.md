## 1 — `rustfmt` is not enforced, and nobody runs `cargo fmt`

*Decided 2026-09-06. Moved here verbatim from [embarch.md](../embarch.md) §5 on 2026-09-10 by `tasks/suite/008`; only the two relative link paths were re-based for this file's depth. Three separate rounds of decision text had landed in that bullet (`suite/006` wrote it, `suite/007` added the two-spellings analysis, `api/024` falsified one of its sentences), and leg 017's closing note asked that the next thing to land there force the question rather than be absorbed too.*

**`rustfmt` is not enforced, and nobody runs `cargo fmt`** (decided 2026-09-06). No gate checks formatting — not [the protocol](../../embarch-fleet/protocol.md) §10, not any repo's CI — and none is being added yet. **This is a sequencing decision, not a verdict on formatting**, and the honest form of it is: adopting is worth doing, doing the expensive half first is not. Measured across the six Rust crates on that date: `cargo fmt` would rewrite **87 files, 1,289 hunks, 1,947 existing lines**, largest `embarch-api` and `embarch-study-designer` at 24 files each. `embarch-outpost` and `embarch-dev-bench` have no `Cargo.toml` and are not in scope. **Formatting without a check decays**: `embarch-umbrella` went 172 → 212 hunks over eleven commits in thirteen hours, monotonically, on 2026-09-05. So the format-the-world commit buys nothing durable until the check exists, and **the check that makes it stick belongs in §10, which is the repo owner's alone.** The `git blame` objection is *not* the reason — `.git-blame-ignore-revs` answers that cheaply, and nothing similarly cheap answers decay. **Reversal condition: the moment a formatting check is added to §10 or to any repo's CI, the format-the-world commits land in the same sitting — one per crate, separate from any behaviour change.** **When that check is wired, neither spelling of the command is simply right, and getting it wrong fails silently rather than loudly.** A cargo command run at a repo root reaches **the packages it selects**, and a path-dependency crate that is not a workspace member is not one of them — the same blind spot for `fmt`, for `clippy --all-targets` and for `test` alike. Bare `cargo fmt --check` used to miss `embarch-api/crates/embarch-core-client` entirely — 6 files, 43 hunks — and pass green with them unformatted. `cargo fmt --all --check` reaches those, and it *also* reaches sideways through `path = "../…"` into sibling **repos**, which is why a naive per-repo `--all` sum triple-counts `embarch-study-designer` and `embarch-topology` — so a per-repo gate spelled `--all --check` fails `embarch-api` on another repo's files. **The check must cover every crate inside the repo and nothing outside it, and which spelling does that depends on the repo's manifest**: scope `--all --check` to the repo's own tree, or run `--check` once per in-repo crate. **Workspace membership is what actually closes it**, and `embarch-api` took that route on 2026-09-06 for the clippy instance ([decision 56](../embarch-api/decisions/tests.md)) — so there, bare `--check` and `-p embarch-api -p embarch-core-client --check` now agree on **24 files, all in-repo** (18 plus the sub-crate's 6), where `-p` previously *refused* outright. **`--all` is unchanged by that**: still 57 files, **33 of them outside the repo** — `embarch-study-designer` 24, `embarch-topology` 9 — and the `embarch-topology` reach is *transitive*, through `embarch-core-client`, so dropping the direct sibling dep would not close it. **The two subcommands do not read the same manifest field** — bare `cargo fmt` follows `members`, bare `cargo clippy --all-targets` follows `default-members` — so a manifest carrying only `members` fixes `fmt` and leaves `clippy` blind. **No other repo in the suite is behind this**: `embarch-api/crates/embarch-core-client` is the only nested in-repo crate anywhere in it, swept 2026-09-06 (`tasks/doc/017`). The trap this is meant to close was open until 2026-09-06: a worker that types `cargo fmt` gets a diff dozens of files wide that `check-ownership.py` allows — in a code repo a worker owns the whole tree — and nothing a worker read said not to. It is closed now, in the fleet's own instructions rather than by a gate: the worker agent definition and [the protocol](../../embarch-fleet/protocol.md) §5's contract both forbid the command (`tasks/doc/014`). That is an instruction, not a mechanism, and the reversal condition above is still what buys one.
